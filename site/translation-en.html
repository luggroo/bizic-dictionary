FAQ

Why did it translate only part of the text?

The AI may stop at an unfamiliar word, fail to load the dictionary, or leave out details even when its answer looks complete. Use the follow-up prompt above to check each sentence. Point out the missing sentence or meaning so it knows what to complete.

A missing lookup can often be resolved by rephrasing: try ‘a person walking’ for ‘passerby’, ‘smell’ for ‘fragrance’, or ‘something to drink’ for ‘beverage’. Preserve the original meaning. A failed lookup does not establish that Tujia lacks an expression.

Why are there Chinese words in the translation?

Tujia has Chinese loanwords. A translation may also borrow Chinese where a suitable native word has not been found. Personal names, place names and cultural terms may retain their Chinese spelling.

The lookup may also be incomplete. Ask the AI to check synonyms, phrases and examples for a suitable Tujia expression, and list any temporary Chinese insertions after the translation.

What if it cannot open the dictionary?

Give the AI the error message and ask it to try another reading method. The full dictionary in the repository is compressed. A code tool can download and decompress it; the repository also provides readable shards.

Resolve the reading error by downloading and decompressing the .gz file, or use the repository's readable dictionary shards. Once you can read actual entries, continue translating.
Why is it still searching without giving a translation?

Ask for the words it has found and the sentences already translated, then have it finish the section. Tool errors should be resolved or handled with another lookup method. On first use, lightweight lookup can start immediately if an embedding model and index are not already available.

Show the words you have found and the sentences already translated, explain where you are stuck, then finish this section.
How do embedding search and lightweight lookup differ?

Embedding search finds candidate entries by meaning. Lightweight lookup rephrases concepts with synonyms or simpler words before searching the dictionary. Both require checking meanings and examples. They help retrieve evidence; the AI still translates the sentence.

An embedding program in the repository does not mean a model was used for this translation. Ask: ‘Which lookup method did you actually use? Show a result from this run.’

Can I translate languages other than Chinese?

Yes. Translate in either direction between Northern or Southern Tujia and any other language the model supports. Results depend on its ability in both languages. For poetry and Classical Chinese, ask it to explain the original meaning first so you can check its interpretation.

What if the romanization and IPA do not match?

Point out the word and ask the AI to check its romanization and IPA against the same source record. Sources may use different transcription systems. Ordinary translations use tone-marked romanization by default; request IPA or Ye-style spelling in your prompt if needed.

Why can a sentence be wrong when every word was found?

One spelling may have several meanings, and the first dictionary result may not fit the sentence. Ask the AI to reconsider the word in context, look for similar examples, and check who performs the action and who or what it affects. A dictionary match alone does not establish the intended sentence meaning.

Can I continue in a new conversation?

In the new conversation, provide the repository link, the Tujia variety and the original text. If tools were installed earlier, ask it to reuse the existing installation and dictionary. Include the previous translation and your corrections when revising.

Translation from this run

A thirsty crow found a pitcher with a little water in it.
gǎ lá lǒng zégǎ. gó guán lá lǒng yǐ liē; guán wótū cě qián xié.

The water was too low for its beak to reach.
cě dī xǐtái, āimǒ gó niě záqī cě bō yè tātī.

It tried to tip the pitcher, but it was too heavy.
gó guán 试着 dǒ 倾斜 liē; qiè guán dù xǐtái, āimǒ gó guán dǒ 倾斜 hīcē.

Then the crow dropped small stones into it, one by one.
āiliē gǎ ǎbǎ bíkuī lá bú lá bú mǒ guán wótū bō liáo liē.

The water rose, and at last the crow could drink.
āimǒ cě óng liē; zuíhóu gǎ cě hǔ dēxǐ.
Translation notes
  • Newly composed Northern Tujia, oriented toward Longshan. Forms primarily follow Brassett (2006); guán, dī and zuíhóu come from Brassett (2004).
  • yǐ ‘see/find’ expresses finding the vessel. guán means ‘jar’ without specifying the particular shape of a pitcher. záqī ‘mouth’ refers to the crow's beak in context.
  • 试着 ‘try’ and 倾斜 ‘tilt’ are Chinese insertions; their IPA is shown as —. Their placement is provisional. duán ‘turn over’ and pié ‘overturn’ would suggest overturning the vessel. Tian Zhihui's V+a+pa construction has a trial or mitigating sense; the available Longshan trial-predicate transcription lacked paired tone-marked romanization in the same record.
  • liáo ‘throw/toss’, combined with ‘into the jar’, expresses putting in the stones. The evidence does not distinguish simply releasing them vertically from tossing them in.
  • The meanings ‘too low’ and ‘too heavy’ are expressed through degree marking and explicit consequences. Sentence 2 uses tātī for circumstantial impossibility; sentence 3 uses hīcē for insufficient physical ability; the final sentence uses dēxǐ for ability once conditions permit. It does not additionally state that the crow finished drinking.
  • Romanization and IPA follow the same selected source records. hǔ ‘drink’ uses /xu²¹/, paired explicitly in Brassett (2006), p. 132, examples 167 and 172, and pp. 129–130. The exported fu²¹ in rn454324 is an error retained only in the raw lookup log. Tones are citation forms; connected-speech changes were not inferred. Chinese insertions have IPA —.
